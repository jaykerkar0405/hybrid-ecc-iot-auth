\begin{abstract}
Al-Rasheed et al.\ proposed a hybrid elliptic-curve/symmetric-key mutual-authentication protocol for IoT devices and evaluated it in OMNeT++ without releasing code. We provide an open reference implementation of the protocol, a C port of its device side that runs on an ESP8266 microcontroller, and an OMNeT++ model of the environment the paper states (its Table~II network and Table~III per-operation times). Under those constants the model reproduces the paper's per-session processing cost of $2T_H+2T_{SE/D}$ per side (23.68\,ms by the paper's arithmetic, 23.98\,ms simulated) and the communication sizes of its Table~IV for the nine schemes that have operation counts in its Table~V. We then time the primitives on the ESP8266 and re-run the model with the measured constants, which lowers the per-session cost from 23.98\,ms to 2.25\,ms, and we study a question the paper leaves open: how a server resolves a pseudonym. With a hash-indexed lookup the per-session latency is independent of the number of devices from 100 to 1000; with a linear scan it grows with it. The implementation, the model, the 3,880-run sweep, the raw hardware logs and the scripts are open, and the model source runs unchanged on Linux and macOS.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Lightweight mutual authentication for resource-constrained devices is commonly evaluated by counting cryptographic operations and weighting them with per-operation times. Al-Rasheed et al.~\cite{base} propose a protocol in which devices and servers share long-term secrets from an offline phase and authenticate online with symmetric primitives only, and they evaluate it in OMNeT++ against other schemes. No implementation or simulation project accompanies the paper.

This paper contributes (i)~an open reference implementation of the protocol (Python, AES-GCM, HKDF-SHA256, SHA-256) and a portable C device core that runs on an ESP8266 and interoperates with the Python server; (ii)~an OMNeT++ model of the paper's stated environment, parameterised only by the paper's Tables~II--V; (iii)~a check that implementation and model reproduce the paper's per-session cost and communication sizes; (iv)~hardware timings of the primitives behind the paper's cost constants, and the same model re-run with them; and (v)~a scalability study of the server-side pseudonym lookup, which the paper does not specify.

\section{Protocol and Implementation}
We implement the nonce-based exchange of Section~III of~\cite{base}. A trusted authority (TA) derives each entity's long-term secret $\lambda_X=H_1(ID_X\,\|\,s)$ locally and distributes pairwise keys $k^\star_{ij}$ in an offline phase. In an epoch $t$ a device $V_i$ derives a pseudonym $PID_{V_i,t}=H_2(\lambda_{V_i}\,\|\,t)$, samples a nonce $N_i$, and sends
\begin{gather*}
M_1=\langle PID_{V_i,t},\,C_1,\,\mu_1\rangle,\\
(C_1,\mu_1)=\mathrm{AEAD}_{k^\star_{ij}}(PID_{V_i,t}\,\|\,N_i\,\|\,t).
\end{gather*}
The server resolves the pseudonym to a known device and epoch, verifies and decrypts $M_1$, samples $N_j$, answers with $M_2$ (the same construction), and both sides derive $K_{ij,t}=\mathrm{KDF}(k^\star_{ij}\,\|\,N_i\,\|\,N_j\,\|\,t)$. Replayed messages are rejected by an epoch window per peer. We instantiate AEAD as AES-256-GCM~\cite{gcm}, KDF as HKDF-SHA256~\cite{hkdf} and $H$ as SHA-256, as suggested in the instantiation notes of~\cite{base}. The wire format is a length-prefixed JSON/base64 envelope.

The device side is also written as a portable C core on BearSSL~\cite{bearssl} with the identical wire format. It runs natively against the unmodified Python server (12 of 12 handshakes) and on a NodeMCU ESP8266 over Wi-Fi (20 of 20 handshakes). In every handshake the session-key fingerprints on the two sides were identical and a replayed $M_1$ was rejected.

\section{Evaluation Setup}
\subsection{Environment and constants}
The OMNeT++ model uses the parameters of Table~II of~\cite{base}: a $1500\times1500$\,m area, 100 to 1000 devices, servers at 5\% of the device count, a 10\,Mbps channel, 75.6\,mW transmit and receive power, a 15\,$\mu$s time quantum, and a 500\,m range. Operation times are those of its Table~III (``reference values for the processing time''): $T_H=0.32$\,ms and $T_{SE/D}=5.6$\,ms. Operation counts and message sizes come from its Tables~IV and~V, and no parameter is fitted to any result. The model is written in plain OMNeT++~\cite{omnet} 6.4.0 (no INET, no SUMO) and is our own implementation of the stated environment.

\subsection{Assumptions the paper does not fix}
The paper leaves several inputs open, and we state ours: an adversary share of 5\% of the devices, each sending forged requests at a mean rate of 1\,Hz; an authentication window of 10\,s in which every device authenticates once at a uniform time; every server resolves every device in its range; and a first-come-first-served server CPU. Devices with no server in range cannot authenticate and are counted separately. Each result is the mean of five seeds. The assumption most relevant to the protocol is how a server resolves a pseudonym (Section~\ref{sec:lookup}).

\subsection{Hardware testbed}
Primitive timings were taken on a NodeMCU ESP8266 (Tensilica L106, stock 80\,MHz and 160\,MHz, about 80\,KB RAM, no cryptographic hardware, Wi-Fi off) with BearSSL, timed by the cycle counter; each figure is the median of 100 runs (24 for elliptic-curve operations). Every benchmark output (hash, AES block, CTR, GCM ciphertext and tag, curve points) was checksummed, and all 35 digests are identical on both clocks and on a laptop running the same code. The whole benchmark was repeated on a second ESP8266 board: the 35 medians agree with the first board and with the earlier run to the microsecond.

\subsection{Simulation with measured constants}
Re-running the model with the ESP8266 constants (Table~\ref{tab:session}, right) puts the per-session cost of the proposed scheme at 2.25\,ms with Table~V's counts and 3.51\,ms with the session KDF, against 23.98\,ms and 35.18\,ms with Table~III. Communication cost is unchanged because it does not depend on processing times. The model structure is the same in both runs, and only the per-operation times differ.

\subsection{Scalability and the pseudonym lookup}\label{sec:lookup}

With the hash index the latency is flat at the cost-model value. With a linear scan, latency grows with the number of devices a server must resolve, and the spread across seeds is large near saturation (standard deviation 2141\,ms at 1000 devices with Table~III constants, 370\,ms with the ESP8266 constants). Faster primitives lower the curve by an order of magnitude but do not change its shape. On a laptop-class server our Python implementation resolves a pseudonym in 4.8\,ms at 1,000 devices and 23.9\,ms at 5,000 (13-epoch window, worst case: device enrolled last); a precomputed table avoids the scan at the price of $N\times W\times32$\,B, 416\,KB at 1,000 devices. The choice is therefore one a deployment must make explicitly, trading server time for storage.

\subsection{Reproducibility}
The model's 3,385-run main sweep was run on two toolchains (OMNeT++ 6.0.3 on Linux and 6.4.0 on macOS arm64); all rows agree to a relative difference of $5\times10^{-14}$. The 495 hash-index and uncontended runs were added on macOS only. The hardware benchmark is archived with raw serial logs for both boards and both clocks, and the C device core interoperates with the Python server on the same wire format. Scripts, configuration generators, result tables and the figure script are open.

\section{Limitations}
The model is ours, not the authors'; the assumptions of Section~III-B are not specified by the paper and results at saturation depend on them. The results of Sections~IV-C, IV-E and IV-F are model output, not measurements, and the ones under Table~III constants use the paper's assumed values. Hardware timings come from one microcontroller family with software cryptography and one library; other devices, libraries or hardware AES will differ, and 8- and 16-bit parts were not measured. The end-to-end firmware figures are means over 20 handshakes recorded in our project repository; the per-primitive benchmarks, unlike them, are archived with raw logs. Constants without a measured counterpart ($T_C$, $T_{fe}$) are taken from~\cite{base}. We reproduce the quantities derivable from Tables~II--V and do not evaluate the figures of~\cite{base}, whose underlying data is not published; the other schemes of its tables are not re-implemented.

\section{Conclusion}
We provide an open implementation, an ESP8266 port and an OMNeT++ model of the protocol of~\cite{base}. Under the paper's stated environment and constants, they reproduce its per-session processing cost and its communication sizes. Timing the primitives on a constrained device and re-running the model with the measured constants lowers the per-session cost by an order of magnitude, and the server-side pseudonym resolution, which the paper leaves open, determines how the protocol scales with the number of devices.
